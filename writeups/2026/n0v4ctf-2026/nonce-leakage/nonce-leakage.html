Nonce Leakage.

Examine exposed ECDSA nonce bits and test a candidate flag against the ciphertext.

CryptographyMediumChallenge by NMinhCR7

Inspect the signatures

The challenge supplied eight ECDSA signatures on secp256k1, a public signing key, and a leak field for each signature. All eight signatures verified, and each message digest matched the SHA-256 hash of its transaction text. Each leak exposed the upper 248 bits of a secret signing nonce. Only eight bits remained unknown, leaving 256 possible values per nonce. The distinct r values ruled out exact nonce reuse among the supplied records.

Test the ciphertext structure

The archive included 39 bytes of ciphertext. I tested the hypothesis that the flag was XORed with a repeating 32-byte mask. Under that hypothesis, XORing ciphertext bytes 32 positions apart cancels their shared mask byte. The expected NVR{ prefix then implies _ecd at positions 32 through 35, counting from zero. Working backward from the likely ending _ecdsa} gives NVR{par at the start.

Check the candidate

The candidate fits the ciphertext length and satisfies all seven overlapping byte pairs. Those checks constrain the ends of the flag. The middle wording is an inference from the challenge theme, and the supplied files give no encryption or key derivation procedure to verify it.
