ISACA Cybersecurity Case Study Competition.

I developed a case analysis and practical plan for safer software deployment and stronger cybersecurity governance. My team, CyberMade, won first place.

1st place31 teams · 148 students · 15 institutionsInternational competition

The result

CyberMade won the 2026 ISACA New York Metropolitan Chapter Cybersecurity Case Study Competition and received a first-place team award and academic scholarship.

After our initial case presentation, we advanced to the final round and faced Hack to the Future in a live championship debate.

The case

The assigned case examined the July 2024 CrowdStrike global IT outage. A defective Falcon configuration update caused Windows systems to crash and disrupted airlines, healthcare, financial services, and other critical operations. I examined the technical failure alongside weaknesses in validation, change management, vendor oversight, and business continuity.

The central question was how an organization could keep a trusted vendor’s mistake from becoming an enterprise-wide crisis.

The approach

I treated configuration changes as operational risks, even when they did not modify application code. My recommendations combined risk-based testing, stronger release approval, ongoing telemetry, and post-implementation review.

  • Stage deployments: validate internally, release to a small canary group, check stability, then expand in controlled steps.
  • Limit propagation: segment rollout groups and give operators a way to stop an unsafe update.
  • Plan recovery: maintain rollback and business continuity procedures for critical systems.
  • Govern vendors: set clear expectations for testing, accountability, incident response, and recovery.

I focused on controlling the blast radius of a failed deployment: the scale of harm depends on how quickly and widely a defect spreads. I used the NIST Cybersecurity Framework to connect deployment safeguards with management accountability and organizational resilience.

The final debate

The championship introduced a new question: how should executives govern cybersecurity and third-party risk as AI accelerates the pace of change? I developed Operating Safely in an AI-Accelerated Risk Environment to connect vendor risk, continuous change governance, deployment safety, and resilience engineering.

Each team delivered opening arguments, critiqued the other team’s position, and answered questions from industry judges. The format required us to defend specific management decisions, not just describe the technology.
